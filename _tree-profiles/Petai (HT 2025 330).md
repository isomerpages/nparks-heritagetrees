<p>The Petai is a tall, evergreen tree with an umbrella-shaped crown, growing up to 15 – 45 m in height. Classified as ‘Vulnerable’ in Singapore, its natural distribution ranges across Southeast Asia from southern Thailand through Peninsular Malaysia, Sumatra and Borneo. The tree has a smooth trunk with reddish-brown bark and large buttresses. It has feather-like compound leaves with small leaflets (3 – 12 mm long by 1 – 3 mm wide), and brown-yellow flowers, which form round heads on long stalks (20 – 50 cm long).</p>

<p>The flattened pods, which can reach up to 51 cm long, contain edible seeds. The seeds have a distinctive garlicky scent and flavour that some find unpleasant, earning the tree its other common English name, Stink Bean. Commonly used in Southeast Asian cuisine, they are  eaten raw, cooked or roasted as a side dish with rice. The Petai produces timber that is used in various ways, including light construction, interior finishes, carpentry, and making plywood, furniture, crates, clogs, matches and disposable chopsticks.</p>

<p>This tree stands near the <i>keramat</i> (shrine) of Sultan Iskandar Shah, the fifth and last king to rule Singapore in the 14th century. The structure features 20 wooden pillars supporting the roof, each carved with fighting cock motifs of Javanese origin.</p>

<p>This tree had a girth of 3.5 m at 1.6 m height when it was endorsed as a Heritage Tree in 2025.</p>
